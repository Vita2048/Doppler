Source starts stationary, accelerates from t=4 to t=5, then moves at half the wave speed past a stationary off-path microphone. Wavefronts originate at their emission positions. Travel time is included: t_receive = t_emit + distance(t_emit)/v. Audio phase and predicted frequency use that same retarded emission time. Rings are representative, slowed wavefronts, preserving relative spacing; they are labeled schematic. The observed frequency is f_recv = f*v/(v-u_r), where u_r is source velocity toward the mic at emission. This reduces to the head-on formula but remains valid for the off-axis fly-by.

The Doppler effect changes the frequency the mic receives. The spectrum shows that change as a moving peak. The diagram is labeled predicted received frequency, not a measured FFT. The final history strip plots f_recv against received time for the already-heard pass; no tone replay. Both panels freeze at the same approaching example for the formula explanation.
